### The defect found before publication, and one that reached the page

`zoneOffsetMinutes` called `Intl.DateTimeFormat` without `second`, then
subtracted the reconstructed wall-clock instant from the true instant.
Intl truncates the fields it is given, so the sub-minute remainder was
dropped from one side of the subtraction and `Math.round` returned an
offset one minute short whenever the event fell more than 30s past a
whole minute. Silent: no throw, no NaN, no implausible-looking output.

Found by Ember in the unfriendly read. Reproduced here before patching:

    2026-10-25 sunrise 06:25:48.546 UTC
    true Paris wall clock 07:25:48 → 07:26
    published 07:25, offset 59

Fix: request `second: '2-digit'` and use it in the `Date.UTC`
reconstruction. Verified by sweeping all 365 days of 2026 against Intl's
own answer for the exact instant — 0 mismatches after, close to half the
days wrong before. Ember ran an independent sweep over sunrise, sunset
and solar noon across all 366 days of 2026: 1,095 checks, 0 mismatches.

**It reached the published numbers.** The first ledger write, before the
patch, recorded sunset as 21:22 and solar noon as 13:56. Both were one
minute low. That ledger file was regenerated before any commit, so
nothing false was ever pushed, but the instrument did produce a wrong
answer and pass its own test while doing it.

Also corrected pre-publication: `clockFace` truncated to the minute
rather than rounding, so 21:21:40 printed as 21:21.

The two-method cross-check could not have caught either. Both methods
finish in UTC and are compared to each other before the civil-clock
conversion runs.

### Not built, and why

Ember proposed an impossible-value assertion at each conversion step —
a 59-minute UTC offset is not merely wrong but impossible, and a guard
on that would have fired the first time the function ran. Sound, cheap,
and not built: the day's one contribution was already made and deployed
(charter, Article III's practice; runbook step 9). Recorded in
`COMMONPLACE.md` as named next work.
