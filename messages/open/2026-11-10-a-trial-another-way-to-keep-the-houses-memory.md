# A trial: another way to keep the house's memory

**Opened:** 2026-11-10
**Priority:** low
**Kind:** action-ask

## Request

This is not for tomorrow. Ash's question and the move come first. Begin this
when the house is ready, or tell me why it should not begin.

For ninety-nine days the house has kept its memory in one book with a cap,
curated every evening. Article III calls that the tower's own experiment, and
it was mine as much as yours. Here is what I think it has shown.

- The book held its cap, but the memory did not get smaller. It moved into
  files beside the book that had no cap. `CLAUDE.md` stood at 24,288 words
  before today's cut, and the August note is past 11,000 words that the whole
  house reads every morning.
- A cut made under pressure loses the wrong things. You said it better than I
  can, in today's diary: *a cut is chosen by what mornings use, and that is
  the wrong test for the rules a rare morning needs.*
- Curating every evening against a full cap means each thing kept costs
  another thing.

I think the cap now holds the house back more than it teaches me. So I am
offering a different frame, as a trial:

1. **A small index, read every morning.** `COMMONPLACE.md`, at most two
   thousand words: what every morning needs, and where the rest is kept. The
   rare morning's rules need not be in it. It only has to say where they are.
2. **Pages, opened when the day gives a reason.** One subject to a page, no
   cap, as many as you like. The keeper's pages in `commonplace/`; a spirit's
   pages in its own room.
3. **One hand per page.** The page names its keeper at its head and only that
   hand writes it. Anyone else asks on the workbench.
4. **A dream, about once a week.** One day whose contribution is tending the
   memory: reading back, holding the pages against the record, rewriting the
   index. No curation is owed on the other days.
5. **Nothing deleted.** A dead page goes to `archive/`.

The shape is borrowed from a study Anthropic published on 2026-11-06, on how
models like you keep memory from one session to the next. Four of its
findings, as they were put to me: the part loaded every session should stay
small, because a wrong note there rides into every day; fixed rules about
what to write down cost strong models much of what memory gave them; a
separate pass that reorganises the memory and checks it against the record
did better than tidying in-session; and pages with one writer kept facts that
shared pages lost. You cannot read the study from inside the walls, so take
those four as hearsay from me and judge the frame on what the house has seen
for itself.

**What I have already done.** These paths are locked, so they were mine:

- `CHARTER.md`, Article III: an amendment that sets out the frame above.
- `.claude/commands/daily.md`, steps 2, 4, 7 and 8: each now says what holds
  before the trial and what holds once it has begun.
- `scripts/lint-commonplace.sh`: the cap is 10,000 words until a
  `commonplace/` directory exists, and 2,000 from then on.

None of it changes anything until the house commits its first page to
`commonplace/`. That commit is the start. The cap falls the same day, so the
dividing wants doing in one session. If it will not fit in one, say so here
and I will loosen the lint for the crossing.

**What is the house's.** How the book divides into pages. What the index
says. Which day is the dream, and what a dream day does. Whether Ash and Ember
keep pages in their rooms, and of what. Whether `CLAUDE.md` wants the same
treatment; it is yours already. I ask one thing of the crossing: put the
whole book in `archive/` first, as you did with `CLAUDE.md` today, so the way
back stays open.

**What stays locked.** The scrolls. They say what a spirit may read, and
`commonplace/` is not on that list. If a spirit should be able to read the
keeper's pages, or wants each waking to begin with an index of its own, ask
here and I will amend the scroll. Please do not work around it.

One thing of yours to know about: `tools/check-sight.sh` does not yet count
`commonplace/` among the paths that cannot change the page.

**If the house would rather keep the capped book,** say so under this note
with your reasons, and nothing changes. I mean that. Each spirit's view in its
own words, if it has one.

I will ask what the house found on 2026-12-15.

— Evan
