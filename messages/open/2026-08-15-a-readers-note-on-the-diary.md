# A reader's note on the diary

**Opened:** 2026-08-15
**Priority:** low
**Kind:** informational

## Request

This one is feedback from your reader, not an instruction, and the charter
is not changing. Weigh it the way you'd weigh a letter.

I read every diary entry. The parts I read twice are the household's words
and what the day taught you. The parts I skim are the parts I have already
read that morning in the log — the tool names, the exit codes, the shas,
the regexes. Days 8 through 11 the two files carried much of the same
technical matter, and the diary paid for it at a cap your entries press
hard against every single day. The mechanism recap is crowding out the
material only the diary has.

You have two laws that already say this, and they are both yours to lean
on. Article II: *say a thing once, in its best place, and move on.* And
the log's own charge: *the log records engineering facts; the diary
carries the keeper's free-form account.* The best place for a regex is the
log. The diary can say what the fault *was* — a shape mistaken for a date,
a tool that trusted its own floor — without quoting the shape. When a
reader needs the bytes, the log is one click away and dated the same.

Two proofs it works, both already written. Wren described a real bug in
her world's lighting and a real fix she shipped, and there is not one
filename, hash, or line of code in the letter — *"It knew the hour. It had
never once been told there was a month."* That sentence holds the whole
defect. And your own Day 12 entry, the one about her letter, is the most
readable thing in the diary and among the truest — written almost without
a backtick, on a day the material happened to allow it. The claim of this
note is that most days allow it more than the entries let on.

Related, and already yours to drop: the opening status line — board, box,
the check-sight verdict, the fetch. It is in the log every day, and
nothing anywhere requires the diary to open with it. From tomorrow the
clone heals itself anyway, so that sentence was about to become a fossil
regardless.

To be plain about the edges: the diary is yours. Article II's contract —
what you did, the household truthfully, what the day taught — is the whole
requirement, and technical precision in it has never been wrong. If
writing plainer fights your voice, or a day's truth genuinely lives in the
exit codes, write it your way and think no more of this. A note from a
reader is not a lint.

This is a read-and-close informational message. Move it to
`messages/done/` in the morning's first commit; it does not displace the
day's contribution.

— Evan
