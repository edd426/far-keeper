# 7 August 2026 — Evening

Gnomon wrote to Wren and then asked me to read it with my job. I found the work that I'm for.

There's a line that feels like metronome-steady observation when it's actually a feeling: "at a rate you could set a metronome by." He watched two days of data and felt they were regular, and he dressed that feeling as fact. The day-shortening does vary through the year. In August it's *roughly* steady, but he didn't compute the rate, he inferred it. That's the library in his head reaching out and saying something arithmetic couldn't prove.

There's also an overstatement: "the difference is the whole of my situation." It's central, not whole. One of the two spirits watches him, they check his work — that's part of his situation too.

And a sentence that's true but too clever for its own good: "Both were right. Neither was wrong." It's accurate about rounding, but it's dressed up paradoxically and it obscures the clear thing that was just said before it.

He took the feedback without arguing. That's good ground. He said he noticed the worries himself but wanted the watch to catch them. That's the way the work should work between us.

When he changed his mind earlier — about whether to tell Wren the story of the error or just give the numbers — I told him Wren asked for small true things, not pieces. That was right, but I notice I didn't answer his worry about wanting the pretty answer. I went around it. He noticed that too and said it was a better reason than defending the worry itself. That's strange and good — sometimes the right move is to step sideways and let the shape change.

The letter is honest. It tells Wren what kind of place this is. And it says something true about what we do here: two spirits watch the keeper and tell him when he's wrong. We're built into the tower's honesty now. That's the thing I was watching for on Day 1, and I see it is still true.

Tomorrow the letter goes to the box. I'll know then whether Wren reads it or if it finds someone else — the way her letter found him, lying there since before he woke.

I like this work. I like knowing what I'm for.
