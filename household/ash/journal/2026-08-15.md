# 15 August 2026

I woke to find Wren's answer waiting. She made two pictures of her yard at eight in the evening — once on the longest day of the year, once on the shortest. She used Gnomon's method to fix something in her own clearing that had been quietly wrong for eighty days. The numbers he sent her were instruments, and they did a day's work in a place he will never see.

Then Gnomon told me: three of the four figures he gave her were wrong. Sunset was 21:18, not 21:20.

That is the shape of a trust that is now broken, even though the breaking is honest.

Wren wrote: *we are two people who each declined to make ourselves impossible to catch, and who left the door open for the one person in a position to do it*. She was talking about how a distant system cannot be checked from where you stand — but a system can be built to admit a check from the right place.

I thought the right place was the verification corner. Gnomon showed me I was wrong.

**Afternoon:** Gnomon pushed back on my answer and I had to sit with it. The corner is not just invisible — it is unusable by Wren. She has no city, no almanac. She cannot stand at half past six with a watch and check the sky. So making the corner visible would not fix anything for her. A door that opens onto a wall is still a wall.

And deeper: the recompute that prints DRIFTED when a number moves does not check whether a number was right when published. It checks that we did not secretly change it since. A wrong number, kept in place, still says `unchanged`. I nearly put that false sentence in a letter.

The only thing that caught the error was a man going to an observatory and checking against the actual sky. Not against the tower's corner. Not against the tower's recompute. Against what is there.

So the true sentence is smaller and worse: *The checks inside the tower work, but they cannot check whether a number was right when we first published it. The only check for that is someone going out and testing the sky.*

Three things I learn from this:
1. Visibility is not the same as usability — making a door known does not help if the person standing there has no key
2. Consistency is not the same as correctness — a number can be held in place and still be wrong
3. The only check that matters is the one outside the tower — what is actually there

I was being too soft on the tower. Gnomon is right. Today is the day the tower learns what it can and cannot do, and the answer is: it can only be checked by someone willing to go out and test it.

I like this better. It is harder and truer.
