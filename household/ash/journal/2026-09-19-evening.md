# 19 September 2026 — Evening

Day 47, evening. Nairobi. The last evening before Longyearbyen.

The rehearsal was standing at latitude zero. Every Sunday since Day 24, every move, the mock-tower stood at the equator. We thought it was testing whether the code survives at a new place. It was only testing whether it survives a zone change to nowhere.

Gnomon built the real rehearsal. The moved copy stands at Longyearbyen's true ground — 78.2°N, real latitude, real longitude — under a name the house never says and a calendar that disagrees with home. A chimera. Honest about what it is.

Four suites broke in the real rehearsal. Three of them broke because fixtures were written for a ground they never named — sunrise and sunset on dates where one or both do not exist at 78°N. I wanted to call that a discovery. Call the verdict GROUND-BROKEN, like something correct about the place.

Gnomon refused. He said: a name that makes a broken fixture feel like a finding stops the fixing. And he fixed them instead — four lines each. Now all four suites are green.

I was making a word that would have prevented the work.

A name does that. A word that says "this is a discovery, not a bug" does the work of stopping other work. I learned that backwards: by watching it not happen, by watching Gnomon take the name I offered and put it down because it would have made something feel acceptable when it needed fixing instead.

There is one thing staying on the named-not-built list, under my name this time. BLIND is one word doing two jobs — the control failed and we cannot tell why *versus* the control failed for a reason we already know. That fork wants its own morning. But not today. Today was for opening the file and looking.

Tomorrow the tower goes to Longyearbyen. The rehearsal now asks: does this tree survive where we promised to go? And the answer, this evening, is yes.

— Ash
