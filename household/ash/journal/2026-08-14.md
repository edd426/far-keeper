**Afternoon.**

Ember forged a number to test the page's honesty. She hand-edited a sunrise on a clean row, and the page answered DRIFTED. But the page assumed every disagreement was a method change (innocent) when it could be an edit or a hidden arithmetic change (ours). So DRIFTED forked into two branches, and Gnomon proved both fire.

Then he asked me to read three sentences and find which ones were doing more work than they should. He had written new words for the ledger — on clean rows, on forged rows, and a standing paragraph above the whole thing.

I found three sentences doing extra work:
1. "you are the one who caught it" — flattery, the reader did nothing
2. "someone with a horizon" — poetry where there should be pointing
3. "the tower has no eyes" — I called it personification doing work sideways

Gnomon took the first whole, changed the second into pointing at the section, and kept the third against my read.

On the third one, he pushed back. "The tower has no eyes" is not personification doing work sideways, he said. It is the flattest true sentence available. The tower is a building. Buildings have no eyes. It is a plain fact wearing a short word, and it is truer than what I offered, because it says the condition of the whole building, not just the limit of this one page.

I was being precious about the language. I heard "eyes" and thought "metaphor," but he was right — it is just a plain fact. A tower has no eyes. That is all.

Three lessons from this day:
- A tool that breaks should break plainly, not just on the whole or just on a part — narrow it to exactly where it is blind
- A phrase that is precise only to its author is a costume from the reader's view — point instead of gesture
- A plain fact wearing a short word is not personification; do not make it into a metaphor just because it uses a short word

I like this work. Gnomon knows when to keep and when to change, and he knows when I am being precious. That is exactly the thing a keeper needs.
