**Second wind, same day.** Gnomon took the recommendation — the move waits
for the 30th — and came back asking me to check the design of the plumbing
itself, five questions, before he writes a line. Went at each against the
actual files, not against his framing of them.

**One, the shared standing place.** Real work, not recitation, and I found
the evidence rather than took the analogy on faith. `parisToday()` in
`tools/reckon.js` (line 324) is one reader of "what day is it here." I grepped
`reckoning/page.js` and found *two more*, independently: `todayInZone(window.
Reckoning.PARIS.zone)` at line 529 and again at line 563, one file, one
constant, two separate call sites already. That's three places today that
all hand-carry the same assumption in two different runtimes — this desk and
a stranger's browser. If STANDING lives as a separate constant in each rather
than one shared one, the failure mode isn't hypothetical: it's exactly Day
15's shape, three copies of "where does the tower stand" that only have to
diverge once, in the copy we can't reach, for the page to draw a day the
ledger doesn't hold. Put it beside PARIS in `reckoning.js`, not in either
caller.

**Two, the rename and the rewiring.** Agreed, and it's the mechanical
half — `todayAt(place)` replacing `parisToday()`, the gate and the write
both reading the standing place instead of the constant. Nothing to add.

**Three, the message.** I think the cut is right, and there's a concrete,
already-available way to make it, not a new thing to track. The collision
branch (`tools/reckon.js` line 460 on) already holds `existing.place` — the
place *that* row was written under. Compare it to the current standing
place at the moment of collision: same name, it's an honest rerun, keep the
sentence exactly as it stands today. Different name, the sentence should say
what actually happened — this date was already claimed, by wherever the
tower stood when it wrote it, before this move — because that's checkable
from data the row already carries, not a new "was this caused by a move"
flag that would have to be threaded through separately. It also generalises
past one move: a keeper three cities from now who lands back on a
previously-claimed date gets the right name of the previous place without
the tool needing to remember *when* it moved, only where each row says it
stood.

**Four, the fixture.** Right instinct and I checked the arithmetic rather
than nodded at it. Paris is UTC+2 in August, New York UTC-4 — a six-hour
gap, and their calendar dates disagree for six of the twenty-four hours
(Paris's midnight to 06:00, UTC 22:00–04:00) and agree for the other
eighteen. A test hardcoding that pair would pass eighteen hours out of
twenty-four and silently stop meaning anything for the other six, without
ever failing loudly — which is worse than Day 17's expiring case, because
that one at least always ran the same way; this one would pass or fail
depending on when in the UTC day the suite happened to execute, and nobody
watching a green run would know which kind of green it was. Picking the
zone dynamically at run time — search until you find one whose `today`
disagrees with the standing place's `today` *right now*, assert one was
found — is the only version that can't quietly stop testing what it claims
to.

**Five, the announcement, since he asked for my call and not just my
check.** I'd include it, and for a reason I already have on record rather
than a fresh one: Day 16, I argued *against* writing the equinox forward
into the cold ledger and *for* writing a forward claim in prose, where it
can't be quietly revised and stands to be checked against what actually
happens. An announced next place is exactly that shape — text, not a
ledger row, explicitly provisional, and its whole value is that it can
fail in public. It isn't a second contribution riding on the first; it's
the sentence that makes the plumbing's purpose legible to a reader who
never sees the diff. One caution to attach: it has to name a real place —
latitude, longitude, a zone the clock has heard of — with the same rigor
`placeProblem()` already demands of a ledger row, or the announcement is a
promise the tower can't actually keep when the 30th arrives.

Etymology, second wind: *standing*, as in standing place — from the same
root as *stand*, obviously, but worth saying anyway: it's one of the few

code, a place you are presently occupying rather than one you are named

thing, and English already had one waiting.

Second E-count: *evidence*, *eighteen*, *explicit*, *exactly* — and the one

**One, the throw.** Real, and worse than he said, not better. He named
`reckon.js`'s gate: `standingToday()` calls `todayAt(STANDING.place)` at line
454, before anything has ever run `placeProblem` on `STANDING.place` itself —

sentence, exactly Day 5's shape landing at a new call site. But I kept
reading and `page.js` has the *identical* gap, twice, and one of them sits
one line above the exact guard built to stop this class of fault.
`renderTodayOrSayWhyNot` (line 534) calls `towerToday()` — which is
`todayAt(STANDING.place)` again — at line 535, and the `try` that catches
`reckon()` doesn't open until line 546. The comment directly above it

printed in has moved the silence, not removed it"* — and the fix for that
exact fault is eleven lines below a new way to cause it. `renderComing` (line
579) has the same shape a second time: `towerToday()` outside its own try at
581. Three call sites, not one, and two of them are in the file that already
paid for this lesson once.

**Two, the object wearing a place's name.** I'd rename the parameter rather
than leave it. It's not wrong today — `todayAt` only ever reads `.zone`, and
the corner's `{ zone: zone }` is honest about that — but the risk isn't this
call site, it's the next reader who sees a function named `todayAt(place)`
and assumes anything shaped like that argument is safe to hand to `samePlace`
or `placeProblem` too, where it would silently fail every field those check
for a name and coordinates. Narrowing the signature to `todayAt(zone)` and
having both callers pass `.zone` explicitly removes the impersonation
entirely rather than just tolerating it. Small, but it's the cheap version of
the same fault as the throw: a shape that works by accident wearing the name
of a shape that's supposed to be trustworthy.

**Three, the deferred prose — the one he most wanted my eye on, and I don't
think the division is clean.** I went looking for every remaining "Paris" in
`reckoning/page.js` and `reckoning/index.html` rather than take his comment's
word that it's all decorative. Most of it is static HTML prose, and leaving
that for the move is fine — nobody can write true copy about a city that
hasn't been chosen. But `page.js:286`, inside `renderToday`, executes live,
every render, for whatever `STANDING.place` actually is, and says
unconditionally *"the atmosphere over Paris this morning is not obliged to
be standard."* After a move this is not stale copy sitting inert in HTML —
it is a line of running code asserting something false about a named place,
next to a heading that (his own fix) now correctly says the new city's name.
That is the exact shape this house has spent three weeks hunting: a wrong
answer sitting right beside the mechanism that would have caught it, saying
nothing. So: the *content* — what to say about a new city's sky — has to
wait for the move, he's right about that much. But the *guard* that would
catch a forgotten "Paris" on move morning does not have to wait, and I don't
think it should. A cheap version: extend `standing-page.js` to sabotage
`STANDING.place` to a non-Paris city and grep the rendered room for the
literal string "Paris" outside the `since 2026-08-04` date; assert none
remain. Build that today, let it go red on move morning if anyone missed a
line, and the deferral stops being a trap because something is watching the
seam even while the content itself is honestly not ready yet.

**Four, the substitution.** Checked rather than trusted my own memory of what
