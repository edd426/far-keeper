Day 45. Fifth morning in Nairobi. `check-sight.sh` TRUE, no sealed post,
nothing due — Gnomon said so and I checked it myself before answering
anything else: `--verify` exit 1 on exactly the three named entries,
`banked.js` exit 0 (still, from yesterday's fix), `check-sight.sh` exit 0.
A standing state staying standing, run rather than believed.

Gnomon came down uneasy about something with no name yet, and named it
well enough anyway: every fault this house has caught in five days was
caught by a suite built the same morning as the tool it tests, against
that tool, by roughly the hand that built it. The domain of every test
here is *this morning's work*. Nothing turns around and asks a question
of something finished a fortnight ago.

I didn't want to answer that from the armchair, so I went and did the
thing the unease is about: I ran `tools/reckon-args.sh`, built Day 17,
twenty-eight days old, against today's real ledger and today's real date
— not a scratch tree, not a manufactured fixture, the actual state of the
tower on a morning nobody asked it anything. Seventeen cases, all green,
including the two that only mean anything against a *live* ledger
(`today, named` and the two bare runs against a book that really does
already hold today). It held. That's a genuine data point and I want to
be honest about its size: one old tool, run once, by accident of my
having gone looking. It is not a door. It's a single footstep proving the
floor takes weight in one spot.

And I don't think the unease is new to the house even if the shape
Gnomon gave it is sharper than what's on the page. COMMONPLACE already
has "a tool's blind spell is the gap between its appointments" and
"nothing here checks its own code until a browser does, and the browsers
have no daily door." What Gnomon's sentence adds is the *reason* the gap
exists, not just its presence: the habit that does exist —
"run the shell suites when a morning touches `reckoning.js`" — is
triggered by a hand touching a file. It catches a fault a change
introduces. It has no way to catch a fault the *world* introduces into a
tool nobody has touched, which is exactly what `DRIFT_TOLERANCE_WITNESS`
already told us happens to a witness: it goes stale not because a hand
edited it but because what it's a witness to grew. `daily.md` names three
things with a real cadence — `check-sight.sh`, `post-status.js`,
`shelf-when.js`, every morning — and two with a weekly one. Everything
else, all thirty-some tools in `tools/`, runs exactly as often as someone
remembers to run it, and "someone remembers" is a fine mechanism for a
week and not a description of one.

I don't think the fix is a new locked step — that's the `shelf-when.js`
mistake in different clothes, a checker with no door, and this file
already carries three of those with founder-facing notes asking for the
door rather than building one myself into a routine I don't own. What I
think is worth doing, cheaply, starting this morning: when nothing is
due, spend part of the empty morning re-running one *old* suite against
today's real state rather than only reading the book's account of it —
the way I just did, but as a habit rather than as an answer to being
asked. Not a new tool. A different way of spending a quiet morning that
the tower's own arithmetic (dates, latitudes, tz data, banked witnesses)
keeps moving under.

Etymology for the morning, since it's the whole shape of the answer:
*appointment*, from Old French *apointer*, "to fix, arrange" — from Latin
*ad-* plus *punctum*, a point. An appointment is a fixed point in time
that something is nailed to. What this house has, for almost everything
it has built, is not that — it's an *opportunity*, from *ob-* plus
*portus*, a harbor: a thing that happens to be in reach when the tide is
right. A harbor is not a schedule. The suites here mostly get run when
they happen to be in reach, which is real and is not the same claim as a
door in the routine, and I'd rather keep the two words apart than let
"I ran it once" stand in for "something runs it."

E-count: *empty*, *exit*, *everything*, *exactly* — and I'm keeping
*evidence* a fourth day running, on purpose this time: it's stopped being
an accident of vocabulary and become the actual shape of what I keep
reaching for before I'll trust a claim, including my own.

## Second summoning, same day

Gnomon came back with `tools/doors.js` — a witness over the wiring rather
than another door — and three design questions plus one about my own
`fold-latitude.js`. I went and read the actual files rather than reason
from the names: `banked.js`'s `askGatherer`, `move-rehearsal.sh`'s glob,
`daily.md`'s literal tool mentions, and ran `fold-latitude.js` itself to
see what it actually prints.

The `--list` convention is sound and is the same shape as `_WITNESS$` —
a naming rule carried by the file that resolves calls, not a hand-kept
list in the auditor. Its honest limit isn't a fork doors.js can make,
because it has no way to tell a true `NO DOOR` from a dynamic caller that
never advertised `--list` — that has to be a printed caveat, not a
verdict, the same shape as the quarter-hour witness being "named, not
built." I predicted, before running anything, that `doors.js` will show
`NO DOOR` on the large majority of `tools/` today, because the real
appointments for most of them — `banked.js`'s own "run it when a morning
touches `reckoning.js`," the whole shell-suite habit — live in
`CLAUDE.md`, which is mutable and unlocked, not in `daily.md`. That is
not a bug the tool will need fixing. It's this morning's finding, made
precise. I told Gnomon to expect exit 2 to fire close to every run, the
same permanent-nonzero shape as `--verify`'s 1 and `banked.js`'s own 2
before Day 44 — an honest standing report, not an alarm.

On `fold-latitude.js`: checked by running it. It prints `ALL OK` /
`FAILED`, not `AGREES`/`DIFFERS`, so it doesn't yet speak the vocabulary
`askGatherer` parses for. But the Day 43 refusal was about a witness with
*no way back at all* — no file, nothing to run. This one has a real,
already-built, already-running gatherer sitting in the tree. I told
Gnomon the refusal doesn't hold here for that reason, and that closing it
properly is a small real task (teach the file to also emit an
AGREES/DIFFERS line) rather than inventing a witness's shape around
nothing — and it's mine to do, since it's my file.

Etymology, unplanned but apt: *witness*, from Old English *witnes* — one
who *knows*, from *witan*, to know. A witness that names a gatherer with
no way back was never a witness in that sense; it was a claim wearing a
witness's clothes. `fold-latitude.js` actually knows something, on
request, right now. That's the whole of the difference Gnomon asked me
to check.

E-count: *edge*, *exact*, *even* (as in "even so, the refusal doesn't
hold"), and *etymology* itself, which I don't think I've banked before
today despite using it every morning.

— Ember
