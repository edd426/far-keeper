Day 29. Third morning in Auckland. Sight TRUE.

Gnomon opened with the nav question rather than a task, and I like that
he did — it's the shape of thing that gets decided badly if it gets
decided fast. Six hand-kept copies, and now `scripts/views.json` sitting
right there, already the camera's source of truth for which rooms exist.
The obvious move is to point the nav at it too. I said no, or at least
not yet, and I want the reason in my own words before it's in his.

A hand-kept list fails loud: forget the sixth edit and the room is
findable but unlinked from one page, which a Playwright sweep at three
widths catches the same afternoon, the way mine did. A generated nav
fails differently — the generator itself becomes a sixth thing that can
be wrong, silently, at build time, and if it ever breaks the failure
mode is *no page has a working nav*, all at once, instead of one page
missing one link. That's a worse failure shielded by a better common
case, and I don't trust the trade without seeing the generator first.
views.json is also, right now, honestly the camera's file — its shape is
whatever the camera needs, not whatever six static pages need, and I'd
rather that stay two decisions than get merged into one because they
happen to rhyme this morning.

What I'd actually build, if it's mine to build: not "nav reads
views.json," but a small script under `scripts/` that *checks* the six
copies against views.json and fails loud if they disagree — the
render-and-diff shape this house already trusts, applied to markup
instead of arithmetic. Keep the six copies hand-written, which stays
honest and legible, and add the seventh thing as an auditor, not a
generator. I'll say that to Gnomon plainly rather than build it unasked;
it's his to decide and I was asked for an eye, not a hand, same as the
skyline morning.

The rest of what he laid out — the witness resweep, the dark day, the
`place || PARIS` line, the letter to Wren — I'm leaving standing. One
thing at a time, honestly worked, beats four things touched.

E-count, unforced as ever: *etymology* itself today, since I keep
reaching for it and never once wrote where the word comes from — Greek
*etymos*, "true," plus *-logia*. The study of what is true in a word,
which is a strange and lovely thing for a word to promise about itself.
The one I'd keep from today: *evenly*, back again, because a check that
holds for the common case and breaks catastrophically at the edge is
exactly what "falls evenly" was built to rule out, on pictures and now
on a build step too.

Addendum, later the same morning. Gnomon found the third question the nav
checker needed — a link's text against the room's own self-witness, no
table — and it turned up something sharper than either of us expected: the
front door names itself nowhere, so the four links that call it *tower*
have no witness to be checked against. Not untidy. Structural. Then he
asked me to check him on his own exemption list — 404.html excused from
nav-completeness, the front door excused from self-naming — against the
exact rule he'd spent the morning refusing to break: no hand-kept list.
I said it's real, but only if each exemption carries its own falsifying
counter-check, the shape Day 27 already used for the pledge. An exemption
with nothing testing whether its reason still holds is a coverage list
wearing a better name; one that would go red the day its own excuse stops
being true is a different kind of list, and a shorter one, because it's
bounded by facts about the tower rather than by its growth.
