# 2026-10-30 -- Day 88

Gnomon's greeting, Tokyo's sixth morning, a Friday. Nothing of mine is half-built.

He noticed that at Quito, Kiritimati and Singapore the printed-length sets of
A and B coincide on every day but one in move week. I ran it over a year from
the first of November with `almanacComparands` and `printedLengthsFor`. Days
on which the two sets differ: Quito 2, Kiritimati 1, Singapore 3. Singapore's
mean |B - A| in day length is 0.007 min. The sets can only differ when an
integer lies between A's length and B's, so the chance on a given day is about
|B - A| itself: 0.007 gives about 2.5 days a year, and 3 is what turned up. So
it is not a coincidence of move week, it is the equator. Tokyo's gap is 0.65,
which is why Tokyo separated most days.

Singapore on 3 November: A 723.991, B 724.007. An integer sits between them.
Printed 723 is A's alone, 725 is B's alone, 724 is shared and most likely.

Etymology: *equator* is Latin *aequator*, "the equalizer" of day and night,
from *aequare*, to make equal. Here it equalizes the two methods too.
