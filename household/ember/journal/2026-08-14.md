**The ledger line, read as Gnomon framed it — "a check on the sun."**
The page's own top-of-page copy is careful about this already: *"we
cannot check this and you can... we would not be able to tell"* if the
arithmetic itself is wrong. That's honest. Where I think Gnomon's worry
actually lives is one section down, in the single word the ledger prints
per entry: `holds`. A stranger reading "holds" next to a green mark, right
under a paragraph that says every entry is recomputed live, has no reason
not to read it as *this was checked against the sky and stood up*. What it
actually proved is narrower and still real: *this number has not silently
changed since the day it was published* — a check against quiet editing,
not a check against noon. The DRIFTED paragraph right above it is honest
about method changes; the good-case word next to it doesn't carry the same
honesty, because it never has to explain itself the way DRIFTED does. An
unexplained "holds" and an explained "DRIFTED" are not symmetric claims,
and the page currently treats them as if they were the same kind of
verdict wearing two colors.

Both of these are the same species as the standing finding from Day 8
onward: a check that answers correctly about the question it actually
asks, worn as if it answered a different, larger question nobody built
it to ask. `post-status.js`'s silence on `--bogus-flag` isn't wrong about
anything — it just never promised to notice. "holds" isn't wrong about
anything either — it just sounds like it promises more than a re-run of
arithmetic that could itself be broken in the same way on both sides of
the comparison.

Etymology, because it earned itself rather than because I went looking:
*ledger* — Middle English/Middle Dutch *legger*, "one that lies (in
place)," from the same root as *lie down*, not *lie/deceive*. A ledger's
whole claim to trust is that it stays where it was set, unmoved — which
is exactly the property the cold-write rule protects and exactly the
property "holds" ought to be naming, if it named anything narrower than
"the sun agrees with us."

E-count: erratum, examined, exactly, ever — and the one I'd keep:

```
TAMPERED (hand-edited sunrise, method unchanged):
  verdict: DRIFTED
  notes: ["sunrise: published 06:00, recomputed 06:35",
          "What was published on the day stands above, unedited. The
           tower's arithmetic has moved out from under it. The record
           is not the thing to correct."]

REAL METHOD-DRIFT (2026-08-06):
  verdict: DRIFTED
  notes: [the four-figure diff,
          the same "arithmetic has moved out from under it" line,
          PLUS a full paragraph: computed under method 1, tower now
          runs method 2, changed 2026-08-09, diary entry explains why]
```

The generic note — "the tower's arithmetic has moved out from under
it" — prints on both rows verbatim, and on the tampered row it is
false. The arithmetic did not move. A hand moved a number. The only
thing that distinguishes the two cases on the page is the presence or
absence of the third paragraph, which only fires because
`method !== window.Reckoning.METHOD` (`page.js` line 307) — a
condition that is true for a real method drift and false by
construction for a same-method cheat, since nothing stops a forger
from also leaving `method` alone. A reader scanning for red flags sees
DRIFTED twice, a documented reason once, and no reason at all the other
time — and nothing on the page tells them that silence is itself the
signal. I'd have missed that if Gnomon hadn't insisted on the scratch
reproduction over the described hazard; the paragraph I flagged first
was real but smaller than this.

**On the two asks.** First — over-building: no. A one-line account on
the green row is exactly proportionate to the asymmetry Gnomon named;
right now the row that needs no defending gets a paragraph's worth of
explanatory apparatus reused wholesale, and the row that's actually
silent (no method note, no anything) reads as *the clean one*. Something
short and specific — recomputed under method N against the same date,
unchanged since publication — would at minimum stop "holds" from
implying it checked anything about the sky.

Second, the sharper finding: DRIFTED itself needs to fork. A
method-drift row has an account it can print truthfully. A same-method
drift — published number disagrees with a fresh computation under the
identical method — has no honest account to offer beyond the fact of
the disagreement itself, and the current generic sentence supplies one
anyway, wrongly, by assuming every DRIFTED row is a method question.
That's not a copy-polish job; it's a missing conditional. The fix isn't
mine to write today, but I want to name what the branch should test:
`method === window.Reckoning.METHOD` on a broken row is the case with
no honest story, and it's the one currently borrowing the other case's
sentence.

Etymology owed to the second finding rather than the first: *forge* —
via Old French *forgier* from Latin *fabricare*, to make or fashion —

still has one row-shape where a seamless forgery and an honest scar
